## What it is

Kokpit is the back office and public website of a used-car dealership, built so that nothing is typed twice. A car is entered once. The public catalogue, the window sticker, the daily price list, the feeds for ad portals and the assistant all read that one record.

It is made for the owners and staff of small dealerships, who mostly work standing up, on a phone, with one hand free. The admin opens on what needs doing today, not on a dashboard of charts.

## Where it came from

It began as the system I built for my father's dealership, [Automobili Galerija](/work/galerija/). By September 2026 that system did enough that a second dealership could plausibly use it, and the code had the first dealership's name baked into too many places. I stopped adding features and wrote an architecture document first: what is the core, what belongs to one dealership, and how does a change reach each installation safely. The existing code was imported with its full history and reshaped around that answer.

## Intake: a car enters itself

A dealer has three ways to start a record, and none of them is a blank form.

- **Photograph the registration document.** A vision model reads it. Before the image leaves the server, the fields with personal data are masked; the output is then checked field by field against what a registration document can contain.
- **Upload a vehicle history report.** A model extracts the fields through a forced tool call, and the make and model are validated against a catalogue before anything reaches the form.
- **Pick the car from a catalogue** built from the European Environment Agency's public CO2 registration data.

Photos come from the dealer's phone by scanning a QR code on the screen; there is no cable and no upload dialog.

## Valuation: priced from the market

The price a dealer puts on a car decides how long it stands on the lot. Kokpit proposes one from data, and deliberately keeps the language model out of the calculation:

1. Comparable listings are gathered from the market every night.
2. Each is scored for similarity to the car in question and weighted by how fresh it is.
3. Outliers are trimmed, and a weighted median and range come out.
4. Asking prices are calibrated toward selling prices, and a trade-in margin is worked out.

The engine is backtested against the dealership's own past sales. A model can explain the result in words, but it never produces the number.

## Price history, kept by itself

A Croatian consumer law that takes effect in November 2026 requires traders to show, next to every price, the price that applied on 10 September 2026, and to publish a daily price list. For a dealership that means stickers, ads and a price list that must never disagree.

Kokpit records every price change with its date and derives the rest: the sticker on the windscreen, the line in each ad and the daily list all carry the same two numbers. When a price changes, the admin lists exactly which printed stickers are now out of date.

## The platform: one core per dealership

I chose isolation over a shared database. Each dealership gets its own Postgres project and its own deployment; a per-dealership manifest (name, brand, enabled modules) is compiled in at build time. A leak between two dealers would need a mistake in infrastructure, not just a missing filter in a query.

- **One codebase** in a monorepo: the core app, the kokpit.hr site, a private console, the Showroom gateway, eight shared packages and a Python service.
- **Releases in waves.** A change goes out to one group of installations at a time, not to all of them at once.
- **Proof in CI.** Every commit builds two different tenants and scans the demo build to prove that nothing of the first dealership's identity appears in it.
- **A fleet check** runs every two hours against every installation.

The architecture was not my first idea. I had Claude propose one design and Codex argue against it, and the decision record keeps both positions and the reasons for the choice.

## Guard rails

- Row-level security in the database is the first line of defence, with a dedicated reviewer agent for changes that touch it.
- Environment variables can be read in exactly one module; a lint rule rejects them anywhere else.
- Scheduled jobs fail closed: without their secret they do nothing.
- The migration runner refuses to write to a production database without a second, explicit confirmation.

## Where it stands

Kokpit runs one real dealership, my family's, every day, and a public demo dealership with invented data. It has not yet been run by a dealership outside the family; onboarding the first one is the next step, and I would rather say so than imply otherwise.

[Showroom](/work/showroom/), the photo studio module, is built but not yet switched on in production.
